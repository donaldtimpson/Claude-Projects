An undergraduate course in the Theory of Computation, taken from first principles to the limits of what a machine can decide.

We start with the tools: propositional and predicate logic, set theory and functions, and enough graph theory to work with machines as diagrams. Then the hierarchy, in order — finite automata, nondeterministic finite automata, and regular expressions, with the pumping lemma to prove a language is not regular; right linear grammars, context-free grammars, and pushdown automata, with a second pumping lemma for the languages that are not context-free.

The last third is Turing machines and their variants, the Church-Turing thesis and what it means to define "algorithm" at all, decidable and undecidable languages, and reducibility — ending with proofs that particular problems cannot be solved by any machine whatsoever.

No prior computer science is assumed. The logic and set theory you need are built in the opening lectures.
