### Course Description

An introduction to linear algebra, from the concrete methods of solving linear systems to the abstract structures that unify them. Topics: systems of linear equations and row reduction; matrix algebra and invertibility; determinants; vector spaces, bases, dimension, and rank; eigenvalues, eigenvectors, and diagonalization; orthogonality, projections, and least squares; symmetric matrices, quadratic forms, and the singular value decomposition; and the geometry of vector spaces. The course balances computational fluency with conceptual understanding and short proofs, and draws applications throughout — from computer graphics and Markov chains to regression and image compression.

**Text:** Lay, Lay &amp; McDonald, _Linear Algebra and Its Applications_, 5th edition. The course covers Chapters 1–8 in full.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of the year, you will be able to:

- Solve and analyze linear systems by row reduction, and interpret solution sets geometrically.
- Perform matrix algebra fluently, determine invertibility, and apply the Invertible Matrix Theorem.
- Compute determinants and use their properties.
- Reason about vector spaces, subspaces, linear independence, bases, dimension, rank, and coordinates.
- Find eigenvalues and eigenvectors, diagonalize matrices, and apply them to dynamical systems.
- Use inner products, orthogonal projections, the Gram–Schmidt process, and least-squares methods.
- Diagonalize symmetric matrices, analyze quadratic forms, and compute the singular value decomposition.
- Read and write short proofs, and justify your reasoning precisely.
