## What Apache-2.0 asks of an app that ships these clips

Apache-2.0 places conditions on redistributing the *Work* (here, the model weights and code). The audio clips are outputs of running the model. The license says nothing about outputs, and the model card invites real-world use ("we welcome the deployment of the model in real use cases"). The app ships only audio, not the model, so strictly nothing is required. Crediting the model is still good practice and costs nothing.

**Text for the credits screen:**

> Phonics sounds generated with Kokoro-82M (voice "af_heart") by hexgrad, licensed under the Apache License 2.0 — https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M

A shorter version, if space is tight:

> Speech: Kokoro-82M TTS by hexgrad (Apache-2.0)

## Things I could not verify

- **Training data.** The model card says Kokoro was trained on "permissive/non-copyrighted audio", public-domain audio, audio under Apache/MIT-style licenses, CC BY sets (Koniwa CC BY 3.0, SIWIS CC BY 4.0), and "synthetic audio generated by closed TTS models from large providers". Using another provider's TTS output as training data may have broken that provider's terms. Those terms bound the trainer (hexgrad), not people who use the published Apache-2.0 weights. It is still the one supply-chain caveat worth knowing about. I have no evidence that any voice is a clone of a real person. VOICES.md does not say who or what af_heart is based on.
- **Separate voice license.** I found no voice-specific license file. My verdict that af_heart is Apache-2.0 rests on the repo-level `license: apache-2.0` and the README's "Apache-licensed weights". This is not legal advice.
- **Hybrid clips.** `ph_z`, `ph_v` and `ph_dh` mix Kokoro-derived frication noise with a synthetic glottal voicing bar written in numpy. `ph_s`, `ph_sh`, `ph_f`, `ph_th` and `ph_h` are noise re-synthesized from the spectrum Kokoro produced for that sound. All of that processing is my own code in `scripts/`, with no third-party audio and no added license terms.
